A PERSONAL JOURNAL / MEXICAN × ITALIAN

BETWEEN
TWO WORLDS.

Dos culturas, una identidad.
Two cultures, one identity.

An exploration of identity, heritage and the spaces that shape us. Through my Mexican and Italian roots, I am discovering how two cultures can come together to create something entirely my own.

01 / THE BEGINNING

Two cultures.
One story.

I was born in Xalapa, Veracruz, a city in Mexico surrounded by mountains, greenery and a culture rich in colour, history and tradition. I lived there until I was eight years old, making Mexico the first place I called home. My connection to Mexico and Italy began long before I could understand what either culture meant to me. My father moved from Milan, Italy, to Mexico for work as an architect, where he met my mother. Two different backgrounds came together, shaping the beginning of my story. My childhood, however, was not always simple. My parents separated when I was very young, and my mother eventually moved to New Zealand to create a better future for us. Growing up with a complicated family history shaped the way I understood home, belonging and identity. For a long time, I didn’t fully understand how these experiences influenced my relationship with my heritage. I was connected to two cultures, yet there were parts of both that I had not completely explored or understood. This became the starting point for a personal journey into the places, histories and design traditions that have shaped who I am.

I want to understand how my heritage has shaped the way I see the world, and how these influences can come together in my own creative direction.

02 / NOTES & DISCOVERIES

The journal.

For a long time, Mexico was a place I associated with difficult memories rather than a culture I felt connected to. I have never visited Italy or met my Italian family, and there is still a part of my heritage waiting to be discovered. It is something I hope to experience for myself one day, beyond the stories, history and images I have grown up with. It was only recently, when I returned to Mexico, that my perspective began to change. I found myself appreciating the country in a new way, connecting with its atmosphere, its people, its architecture and the details I may not have fully understood before. I began to see it not only through the memories attached to my past, but as a place with its own beauty and meaning. This experience made me question how much of our identity is inherited, and how much is shaped by the experiences we carry. It also encouraged me to look more closely at the visual languages of both cultures: the colours, materials, textures, spaces and traditions that make each one distinctive. Through exploring Mexican interiors, I became drawn to their warmth, colour, craftsmanship and expressive character. Through Italian design, I began to appreciate balance, materiality, simplicity and restraint. As I documented these discoveries, I started noticing connections between the two that I had never considered before. The more I researched, the more I realised that this was not simply about comparing two different styles. It was about understanding what resonated with me personally, what I felt drawn towards, and how my own experiences influenced the way I interpreted each culture.

03 / THE CONCLUSION

A language
of my own.

I began this journey wanting to understand two parts of my heritage, but along the way, I started to understand something about myself, too. My relationship with both cultures is still evolving. There are experiences I have yet to have, places I have yet to visit and connections I hope to make in the future. I have not experienced every part of my Italian heritage, but I am learning that this does not make it any less a part of my story. Instead of feeling that I need to choose between two identities, I have begun to see the possibility of creating something that reflects both. Mexican warmth, colour and craftsmanship can exist alongside Italian balance, natural materials and simplicity. Their differences do not have to compete; they can inform and complement one another. For me, this project has become a way of exploring identity through design. It has allowed me to look beyond aesthetics and consider how memories, experiences, culture and personal perspective influence the spaces and visual languages we are drawn to. I may not have every answer about where I come from, but I no longer feel that I need to. Both cultures are part of my story, and I can continue discovering what they mean to me in my own time. This is where my own design language begins: somewhere between two cultures, shaped by the things I have experienced, the things I am still discovering, and the person I am becoming.

The aim is to create a design language that reflects my own identity: not simply Mexican or Italian, but a personal expression of both.
